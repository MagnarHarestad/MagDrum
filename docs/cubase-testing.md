# Verify MagDrum in Cubase Pro 15

Use the Windows x64 package for Cubase Pro 15 on a standard Intel/AMD Windows PC. The ARM64 package has not been run on native Windows ARM64.

## Install and load

1. Windows x64 package [v0.1.2-test.275](https://github.com/MagnarHarestad/MagDrum/releases/tag/v0.1.2-test.275), VST3 0.1.2.275, adds automatic kit/sample refresh when the instrument manager closes. It is package-tested but not installed; `.274` remains installed per user at `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Common\VST3\MagDrum.vst3`. The .275 ZIP SHA-256 is `0163BCEB386993E862497273358764C4BD5C8BBB0A0852EAEEC9B2E02D0CFE7A`; module SHA-256 is `C0319107CD9C9534AFF8238A786F192BF4700B6C19694734F0B0C8E0CA8E9B9A`. Package contents, project-state restore and MIDI 0–127 rendering passed in hidden tests. Direct `.nbkt` import, populated Battery kit capture and Cubase in-host behavior remain unverified.
2. To install on another PC, download and extract `MagDrum-Windows-x64.zip`, close Cubase, and double-click `Install-MagDrum-User.cmd`. It installs under `%LOCALAPPDATA%` without UAC and includes the matching `MagDrum.exe` helper. It backs up an existing per-user bundle outside VST3 scan paths. A system-wide legacy copy may require one-time UAC cleanup. If the installer reports `Cubase15.exe` still running, check Task Manager's **Details** tab and wait until Cubase has exited.
3. Start Cubase 15. Open **Studio > VST Plug-in Manager**, then choose **Rescan All**. Confirm that **MagDrum** appears in the instrument list. Select it and verify its version reads `0.1.2.274`. Confirm Cubase shows **Default Kit** as the program name. If Cubase exposes a rename control, rename it, save and reopen the project, then confirm the name persists.
4. Create an instrument track with MagDrum and open its editor.

## Use the plug-in views

1. **Sample Library:** click **Scan samples**, choose **Add sample folder**, and select your drum library folder. The VST scans it in the background and shows progress in its status line; scanning can be cancelled and unchanged files are skipped. Use the always-visible sound-family and drum-category filters for the main browsing flow. Click **More filters** to reveal sound-feature, sort, confidence, source and kit filters; the control shows how many of those filters are active and **Hide filters** collapses the row. The family filter separates **Drum one-shots**, **Drum loops & beats**, **Bass one-shots**, **Bass loops & beats**, **Synth one-shots**, **Synth loops** and **Other loops**. Confirm that kick-type “Bass Drum” samples stay in the drum results; isolated bass sounds and bass beats appear in their respective filters, and synth one-shots do not mix with synth loops. The acoustic map follows the selected family. Find and audition samples in the list or sample map. The map draws representative samples from the complete filtered result independently of the list's 250-row pages; clicking a point outside the current list page should still make that sample available for Preview and Assign. Confirm that similarly sounding samples appear near one another; double-click a point to preview it, move the pointer away to stop preview, use the wheel to zoom around the pointer, and hold the wheel button while dragging to pan. Confirm dots stay inside the map frame at the zoom limits. Right-click a sample row and use Assign to pad for a quick assignment.
2. **Plugin Scanner:** open the **Plugin Scanner** view inside the VST. Click **Scan plugin folders**, select an instrument, then scan its exposed programs or import a `.vstpreset` as a named kit. To choose a kit in a vendor editor, open **Choose kit source... > Open instrument manager**; it opens the selected instrument automatically. For Battery 4, load an `.nbkt` kit in that editor, save its state in the manager, then refresh saved kits in the VST. Configure MIDI notes and velocity levels, then click **Scan kit notes**. The isolated helper performs discovery and rendering outside Cubase; captured samples should appear in the VST's sample library with source, kit, note and velocity details. If a vendor instrument crashes discovery, confirm the scan skips that module, continues with the others and reports the skipped count. Direct `.nbkt` import remains unsupported.
3. **Drum Machine:** select one of the named General MIDI pads. Load a WAV directly or assign a selected library sample. The panel at right shows the pad's waveform and velocity layers. Click the pad to audition it and confirm its button glows briefly. Adjust Gain, Pan, Tune, Drive, EQ and ADSR controls. Sweep Filter Cutoff and Q, confirm Q changes the resonance around the cutoff and that maximum cutoff bypasses the filter. Switch the effect panel between Compressor and Phaser, adjust their controls, and confirm each effect changes that pad's sound without changing another pad. In Cubase's automation controls, locate a pad group such as **Kick (C1)**, enable Write, drag Gain and waveform trim to record their curves, then enable Read and confirm each curve plays back only on that pad. Repeat with another pad and verify parameter automation survives saving and reopening the project.
4. **Sequencer and Cubase transport:** choose a pattern length from 1 to 16 bars and enter hits on all 16 named lanes: Kick, Snare, Closed/Open hi-hat, Tom 4, Ride, Crash 1, Clap, Bell, Crash 2, Toms 1–3, Side Stick, Pedal hi-hat and Cowbell. With the General MIDI profile, the row notes are 36, 38, 42, 46, 43, 51, 49, 39, 53, 57, 50, 47, 45, 37, 44 and 56 in that order; a selected MIDI profile can change the first nine lane mappings. Confirm Bell and Cowbell trigger their own assigned pads instead of another drum category. With Cubase stopped, press **Play** in MagDrum and confirm the pattern previews using the samples assigned to its MIDI notes. Stop it, start Cubase playback from a known bar, then press **Play** in MagDrum; confirm the pattern follows the host position and tempo, loops at its selected length, and stops cleanly. Change Cubase's tempo during playback and check that the sequencer follows it without duplicate or stuck notes. Stop Cubase while MagDrum's Play button remains active; confirm MagDrum returns to its local preview instead of staying idle. Export the pattern, import it into a Cubase MIDI track, then reimport it in MagDrum and compare the hits, length and tempo/meter events.
5. To test external MIDI input, create or play a Cubase part that sends note 36 with velocity 100. Cubase's displayed octave name for note 36 depends on its MIDI octave-numbering preference, so verify the MIDI note number if no sound plays. Test a mapped snare note and one unmapped note as well; confirm the correct sample plays and the unmapped note stays silent.

## Check MIDI editing, mappings, comparison and language

1. In **Edit hits**, select an event and change its drum, articulation, velocity, beat and duration. Confirm the event remains at the edited position and that the pattern updates after saving and reopening the Cubase project.
2. In **MIDI Maps**, select or create a profile, change a drum/articulation note mapping, save it, export its CSV, then import that CSV and confirm the assignments return. Export a pattern containing an unmapped articulation with each fallback policy and confirm the report identifies every substituted or skipped event.
3. In **Sample Library**, select two indexed samples and use **Sample tools > Compare two selected waveforms**. Preview each side, stop playback, and confirm both waveforms remain visible. While that comparison window is open, switch languages from the hamburger menu and confirm its title and Preview/Stop controls update.
4. Switch the main editor through English, German, French, Spanish, Swedish, Norwegian, Danish and Finnish. Check all five views, including the open event editor and comparison window, for updated labels and intact accents; restore English afterward if preferred.

## Check project state and velocity layers

1. Save the Cubase project, close and reopen it, and confirm the sample assignments remain in MagDrum.
2. Load another sample on the same note for velocity 1–63 and keep the first sample on 64–127. Send notes at velocities 40 and 90 and confirm each range selects its assigned sample. Adjust that pad's gain, pan, tune, ADSR, filter, compressor and phaser; save the Cubase project, close and reopen it, then confirm assignments, effect settings and the sequencer pattern return unchanged.
3. Confirm that editing the assignment does not interrupt audio while notes are playing. In the hamburger menu, switch through all eight language choices and check that page names, controls and status messages update without broken accented characters.

## Report the result

Record the Cubase Pro 15 build, Windows version and architecture, whether MagDrum appeared in the plug-in manager, sample format, MIDI note/velocity used, whether audio played, and whether the saved assignment returned after reopening. Include any plug-in scan warning or crash text.

These are manual host-compatibility checks. The automated Windows smoke tests load the built VST3 in a headless scanner, but they cannot substitute for Cubase Pro 15 validation.
